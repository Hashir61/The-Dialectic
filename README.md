# The Dialectic

*An independent journal of essays on politics, philosophy, history, cosmology and literature.*

----
The Dialectic is a space for inquiry into questions that do not resolve .

The philosophical essays concern themselves with the problems that recur across every tradition — the nature of being, the limits of what can be known, the grounding of moral claims, the relationship between language and the world it attempts to describe. Ontology, epistemology, metaphysics, ethics, relativism and skepticism.

The political essays take institutions, power and legitimacy seriously — not to celebrate or condemn them, but to examine the arguments that hold them together and the contradictions that run underneath.

The historical essays treat the past as a site of argument rather than settled fact. What happened is rarely the interesting question. What it meant, and to whom, usually is.

The cosmology essays sit at the edge of what philosophy and physics share — the origin and structure of the universe, the nature of time, the question of what it means to exist inside something this large and this indifferent.

The literary essays treat writing as a form of thinking. They are concerned with what a work argues, not merely what it depicts — with structure, voice, and the choices that separate serious prose from decoration.
